    How it works, and what it can't do

    The prediction

    Both models predict the change in the pump price one week ahead from 12 inputs: recent pump-price moves, crude oil moves over the last four weeks (converted to dollars per gallon), how far the pump markup over crude is from its 52-week norm, and season/holiday flags. Forecast = this week's price + predicted change.

    The explanation

    Linear regression: each input's push is its coefficient times how far the input is from average (exact SHAP for a linear model). Random forest: each tree split's change in average prediction is credited to the input it used, then averaged across trees. Either way, base + pushes = the prediction.

    Limits

    Weekly data only. Supply disruptions, hurricanes, refinery outages and policy changes are not inputs, so the models cannot see them coming and will lag big spikes. Treat the forecast as a planning aid, not financial advice.
